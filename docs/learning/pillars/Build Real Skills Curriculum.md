# Purpose

Every Pillar before this one teaches something universal.

AI Foundations teaches mindset. Think with AI teaches collaboration. Meet Your AI Team teaches selection. AI Workflows teaches process.

Those skills work across every domain, every tool, every task.

Build Real Skills is different.

It teaches nine specific domains — website creation, SEO, writing, marketing, programming, automation, design, video, and business — not as abstract knowledge, but as practiced capabilities that produce professional-grade results using AI from day one.

A learner who completes a domain Track in this Pillar can do work in that domain.

Not one day.

Not after more study.

Now.

AI-first means the learner does not learn the domain first and add AI later. The AI is there from the beginning — helping them plan, build, iterate, and evaluate. The domain skill and the AI skill develop together, each making the other more powerful.

This is how modern professional work actually happens.

This is what this Pillar teaches.

# How This Pillar Is Structured

## Track Architecture

Build Real Skills is organized into nine Domain Tracks.

Each Track is a self-contained mini-curriculum covering one professional domain.

A learner does not complete all nine Tracks to complete this Pillar.

They complete the Tracks that serve their Earn path.

A freelance writer completes the Writing Track and the Marketing Track. A content creator completes the Video Track and the Design Track. An aspiring tech professional completes the Programming Track and the Automation Track.

The Tracks are independent. A learner may complete them in any order. There are no cross-Track hard prerequisites within this Pillar.

## Pillar Completion Rule

A learner completes Build Real Skills by finishing at least one Domain Track in full — all Guides plus the Track Mission.

Additional Tracks are encouraged and available, but one completed Track constitutes Pillar completion.

This rule exists because domain skill is specific. A learner who deeply completes one Track has more real capability than one who shallowly touches all nine.

Depth over breadth.

## Track Metadata

Every Track in this Pillar contains:

- A purpose statement (what this domain enables)
- A transformation (who enters, who leaves)
- 3–4 Guides
- 1 Track Mission with a concrete deliverable
- An Earn Path connection

## The AI-First Rule

Every Guide in every Track must teach the domain skill through AI, not before AI.

There are no prerequisite domain knowledge requirements.

A learner who has never written a line of code can enter the Programming Track.

A learner who has never designed a graphic can enter the Design Track.

AI-first means: we begin where the learner is, and AI handles the parts of the domain that require prior knowledge — so the learner can focus on judgment, direction, and quality assessment.

Guide writers must never write a Guide that assumes domain expertise.

They must assume AI capability — which all learners in this Pillar have by the time they arrive here.

# Track 1: Website Creation

## Purpose

Every professional in 2025 needs a digital presence. A website is the foundational asset — a place where a client, employer, or audience can find you, understand what you do, and decide to work with you.

Most Filipinos who want a website believe they need to hire a developer or spend months learning code.

This Track teaches a different path: use AI to plan, build, and launch a real website without prior technical knowledge — and to iterate on it confidently over time.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Has no website or a neglected one. Believes building a website requires skills they don't have.

**Leaves:** Has a real, live website or landing page — built, optimized, and ready to show the world.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `plan-your-website-with-ai` | Plan Your Website Before You Build It | Concept | 5 min | Critical Thinking |
| 2 | `build-your-website-with-ai` | How to Build a Website Using AI | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |
| 3 | `write-your-website-content` | How to Write Every Page of Your Website with AI | Skill | 10 min | Prompt Practice |
| 4 | `optimize-your-website-with-ai` | How to Make Your Website Work Better with AI | Applied | 10 min | Revision |

**Total: 4 Guides · 40 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — Plan Your Website Before You Build It**

Before touching any tool, the learner defines three things: the purpose of the website (what it must make a visitor do), the audience (who specifically is visiting), and the structure (which pages, in what order, containing what). AI is used to stress-test the structure — not to invent it. The learner's own purpose is the non-negotiable input. Exercise: Use AI to review a draft site structure and identify what is missing or in the wrong order.

**Guide 2 — How to Build a Website Using AI**

Teaches the learner to use AI-assisted builders (such as Framer, Webflow, or similar tools that use AI to generate initial layouts) or to use AI to generate clean HTML/CSS that they can paste into a simple host. No prior coding knowledge required. Focus: giving AI an accurate brief that produces something close to the intended result. Teaches brief-writing for a visual output. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner builds their actual website homepage using AI — from brief to published page.

**Guide 3 — How to Write Every Page of Your Website with AI**

AI-first copywriting for the five most important website pages: Home, About, Services/Products, Contact, and a proof page (portfolio, testimonials, or case study). Teaches the brief structure for each page type. Teaches what makes website copy different from other writing (scannable, action-oriented, specific). Exercise (Tier 2): The learner writes the copy for their About page using a provided brief structure, then revises it to sound like themselves.

**Guide 4 — How to Make Your Website Work Better with AI**

Teaches the learner to use AI to audit their own website: identifying unclear language, missing calls to action, slow-loading elements, and mobile problems. Also teaches using AI to write meta descriptions and page titles for basic SEO. Connects to the SEO Track for learners who want to go deeper. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner pastes one page of their website into AI and asks for a structured critique against three specific standards: clarity, action, and specificity.

## Track Mission: Launch Your Online Home

**Slug:** `launch-your-online-home`
**Deliverable:** A live website or landing page with at minimum: a homepage, an about section, a services or work section, and a contact method.
**Time:** 90 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` — A website is the first thing a freelance client asks for. This Mission makes that ask answerable.
**Badge:** "Live Online"

# Track 2: SEO

## Purpose

Every piece of content, every website page, every professional article is findable or invisible.

SEO determines which.

This Track teaches Filipino learners to use AI to research what their audience is actually searching for, write content that ranks, and audit their own work for the factors that determine visibility.

No technical background required. No access to expensive tools. Just AI and a clear understanding of what search engines actually reward.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Has heard of SEO but has no system for applying it. Guesses at keywords. Does not know whether their content is optimized.

**Leaves:** Uses AI to research keywords before writing, structures content for search and readers simultaneously, and can audit any existing page for improvement opportunities.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `keyword-research-with-ai` | How to Find Keywords That Actually Matter | Skill | 10 min | Experimentation |
| 2 | `seo-writing-with-ai` | How to Write Content That Ranks and Reads Well | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |
| 3 | `audit-a-page-with-ai` | How to Audit Any Page with AI | Applied | 10 min | Critical Thinking |

**Total: 3 Guides · 35 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Find Keywords That Actually Matter**

Teaches the learner to use AI to research search intent — not just keyword volume, but what a person searching a phrase actually wants. Uses AI to generate keyword clusters around a topic, identify long-tail keywords the learner could realistically rank for, and map intent (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional) to content format. Avoids paid keyword tools — AI is the research instrument. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to generate a keyword cluster for their domain or service area, then selects the three best opportunities and explains why.

**Guide 2 — How to Write Content That Ranks and Reads Well**

The two goals of SEO writing — satisfying a search engine and satisfying a human reader — are not in conflict when done right. Teaches the learner to use AI to: structure an article around a primary keyword without forcing it, write a title and introduction that capture the reader in the first five seconds, and use subheadings that address secondary keywords naturally. Teaches the difference between AI-written content that satisfies neither goal and human-directed AI content that satisfies both. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner writes a 400-word SEO article using AI — starting from a keyword they researched in Guide 1 — and evaluates it against three criteria: does it answer the search intent, does it include the keyword naturally, and would a human want to read it?

**Guide 3 — How to Audit Any Page with AI**

The fastest way to improve existing content is to audit it before creating new content. Teaches the learner to paste any existing page into AI with a structured audit prompt that checks: relevance to the stated keyword, clarity of the answer to the search intent, missing topics a searcher would expect to find, and readability for a non-expert. Produces a prioritized list of improvements. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner audits one of their own pages (or a provided sample page) using a structured AI audit prompt and writes a three-point improvement plan from the output.

## Track Mission: Rank Something Real

**Slug:** `rank-something-real`
**Deliverable:** One fully optimized piece of content — published or ready to publish — with a keyword research document showing the target keyword, the intent, and why the learner chose it.
**Time:** 60 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` — SEO writing is one of the most consistently in-demand freelance services. This Mission is the first portfolio piece.
**Badge:** "Search-Smart"

# Track 3: Writing

## Purpose

Professional writing is the skill that underlies almost every other professional skill.

Proposals win clients. Emails build relationships. Content builds audiences. Reports establish credibility. The learner who writes well with AI produces work that sounds professional, specific, and human — not like it was typed by a machine and left unedited.

This Track teaches four writing formats that Filipino freelancers and remote workers need most: emails, proposals, content, and professional documents. AI-first means the learner does not master grammar before writing — they use AI to handle language mechanics while focusing on message, structure, and voice.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Writes slowly, second-guesses everything, and produces drafts that feel generic or uncertain. Avoids writing tasks when possible.

**Leaves:** Produces professional written work quickly and confidently across multiple formats. Has a developing voice that survives AI involvement.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `emails-that-get-responses` | How to Write Emails That Get Responses | Skill | 10 min | Prompt Practice |
| 2 | `proposals-that-win` | How to Write a Proposal That Wins the Work | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |
| 3 | `write-content-that-sounds-like-you` | How to Write Content That Sounds Like You | Skill | 10 min | Revision |
| 4 | `write-faster-stay-good` | How to Write Faster Without Losing Quality | Skill | 10 min | Experimentation |

**Total: 4 Guides · 45 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Write Emails That Get Responses**

Most professional emails fail at the first sentence. They bury the point, over-explain, or ask for too much at once. Teaches the one-email-one-ask rule and how to use AI to apply it. Structure: subject line that earns the open, a first sentence that states the purpose immediately, a middle that gives the reader what they need to say yes, and a close that makes the next step obvious. Exercise (Tier 2): Provide a bad email. The learner uses AI to rewrite it using the structure — then reads both versions and writes one sentence explaining what specifically changed.

**Guide 2 — How to Write a Proposal That Wins the Work**

A proposal is not a list of services and prices. It is a document that makes the client feel understood, confident, and ready to say yes. Teaches the five-part proposal structure: the situation (you understand their problem), the stakes (what happens if they don't solve it), the solution (specifically how you will solve it), the proof (why you specifically), and the ask (what happens next). AI builds the draft from the learner's brief. The learner edits for specificity and voice. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner writes a complete proposal for a real or realistic project brief using the five-part structure and AI. The finished proposal is portfolio-ready.

**Guide 3 — How to Write Content That Sounds Like You**

AI content is recognizable: it uses the same sentence structures, the same filler phrases, the same organizational patterns. A learner who publishes AI content unchanged sounds like everyone else who publishes AI content unchanged. Teaches the learner to use their own sentences, opinions, and experiences as the foundational input — so AI functions as an editor, not a ghostwriter. Teaches the voice brief: a short document the learner creates once and uses every time they ask AI to write content. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner writes three sentences about a topic they know in their own voice, then asks AI to expand the content using only those sentences as the perspective. Compare: does the expanded version still sound like them?

**Guide 4 — How to Write Faster Without Losing Quality**

Most learners who use AI for writing do not write faster — they spend the time they saved on revision. Teaches the habits that make AI-assisted writing genuinely faster: the brief before the prompt, the filter before the handoff, the edit before the publish. Teaches the difference between productive revision (changing what the content says) and unproductive revision (changing how it sounds after AI already made it sound fine). Exercise (Tier 2 + reflection): The learner times themselves writing a 200-word piece from scratch, then times themselves using the AI-first workflow. Compare the times and the outputs.

## Track Mission: Your Professional Writing Kit

**Slug:** `professional-writing-kit`
**Deliverable:** A personal writing kit — four documents: a professional bio (third person), an email introduction template, a proposal template in the five-part structure, and one piece of published content. All four must sound like the same person wrote them.
**Time:** 90 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` — Every freelance platform, client pitch, and professional outreach requires these four documents. This Mission makes them real.
**Badge:** "Pro Writer"

# Track 4: Marketing

## Purpose

Marketing is the discipline of helping the right people find you, understand what you offer, and decide to choose you.

Filipino learners who want to freelance, build a business, or grow an audience all need marketing — but most approach it backwards: they create first and figure out the audience later.

This Track teaches AI-first marketing that starts with audience research and ends with copy that converts, in a sequence that mirrors how professional marketers actually work.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Creates content and services without a clear picture of who they are for. Struggles to describe their offering in a way that makes people want it.

**Leaves:** Uses AI to research audiences specifically, write copy that speaks to a defined person's real situation, and build a content plan that serves a marketing goal.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `know-your-audience-with-ai` | Know Your Audience Before You Market to Them | Skill | 10 min | Critical Thinking |
| 2 | `write-copy-that-converts` | How to Write Copy That Makes People Act | Applied | 15 min | Prompt Practice |
| 3 | `build-a-content-plan` | How to Build a Content Plan That Serves a Goal | Skill | 10 min | Hands-On |
| 4 | `measure-what-matters` | How to Use AI to Know What Is Working | Applied | 10 min | Experimentation |

**Total: 4 Guides · 45 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — Know Your Audience Before You Market to Them**

The most common marketing mistake: describing what you do instead of describing what the audience gains. Before any copy is written, the learner uses AI to build an audience profile: one specific person, their situation, their problem, what they have already tried, what they are afraid of, and what they want their life to look like after solving the problem. This profile is the foundation for every marketing decision that follows. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to build a one-page audience profile for their freelance service or business idea. Then they share the profile with a peer or assess it against this question: "Could I describe this person to a stranger in 30 seconds and have the stranger understand them?"

**Guide 2 — How to Write Copy That Makes People Act**

Copy is not writing. Copy is persuasion. Teaches the learner the three elements that make copy work: specificity (names the person and situation exactly), relevance (makes the reader feel understood), and a clear next step (makes acting obvious and low-risk). Uses AI to generate multiple copy angles for the same offer — the learner selects and refines the one that best fits their audience profile from Guide 1. Covers: headline formulas, body copy structure, calls to action. Exercise (Tier 2 → Tier 3): Tier 2 — rewrite provided weak copy using a given audience profile. Tier 3 — write copy for the learner's own service or offering using their own audience profile.

**Guide 3 — How to Build a Content Plan That Serves a Goal**

A content plan is not a calendar. It is a strategy. Teaches the learner to use AI to map content to the stages of their audience's journey: awareness (they don't know me yet), consideration (they're evaluating me), and conversion (they're ready to act). Each stage needs different content. AI generates topic ideas at each stage from the audience profile. The learner organizes them into a realistic 30-day plan based on how many posts they can actually produce. Exercise (Tier 2): Build a 30-day content plan with at least three pieces at each stage of the audience journey, using AI for topic generation and the learner for selection and scheduling.

**Guide 4 — How to Use AI to Know What Is Working**

Creating content without measuring it is guessing. Teaches the learner to use AI to analyze performance data they already have — comments, shares, reach, click rates — and identify the patterns that explain what resonated and why. Also teaches using AI to write post-campaign analysis: what worked, what to change, and what to test next. No analytics platform expertise required. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner pastes their last five pieces of content (or uses provided examples) and asks AI to identify patterns — what topics, formats, or tones got the strongest response — then writes a three-point "next action" plan from the analysis.

## Track Mission: Your First Marketing System

**Slug:** `your-first-marketing-system`
**Deliverable:** A complete marketing starter kit — audience profile, three-version copy for one offer (headline, social caption, full pitch), and a 30-day content plan with at least nine pieces mapped to the three audience journey stages.
**Time:** 90 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` + `/earn/content-creation` + `/earn` (general entrepreneurship) — Marketing skills are the single most transferable professional skill across every Earn path.
**Badge:** "Market-Ready"

# Track 5: Programming

## Purpose

Programming is no longer exclusively for people who learned computer science.

With AI, a non-developer can write working code, build functional tools, automate tasks, and solve technical problems — by communicating clearly, understanding what the code should do, and being able to evaluate whether it works.

This Track does not teach the learner to be a software engineer.

It teaches them to be a capable technical builder who uses AI to close the gap between what they can imagine and what they can build.

Filipino learners who want to offer development services, build their own tools, or qualify for technical remote jobs will find the most direct path here.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Has never written code, or has tried and stopped. Believes programming requires years of study before producing anything real.

**Leaves:** Has built and shipped at least one working tool using AI. Can write a technical brief that produces useful code, debug errors systematically, and iterate on a working tool without starting over.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `brief-ai-like-a-developer` | How to Tell AI Exactly What to Build | Skill | 10 min | Prompt Practice |
| 2 | `read-and-debug-with-ai` | How to Understand and Fix Code Using AI | Skill | 10 min | Critical Thinking |
| 3 | `build-a-tool-with-ai` | How to Build a Working Tool with AI | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |

**Total: 3 Guides · 35 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Tell AI Exactly What to Build**

The reason most learners fail to get useful code from AI is not that AI cannot code. It is that their brief is too vague. "Build me an app to track my clients" produces a generic result. "Build a Python script that reads a CSV of client names and email addresses, checks which ones I haven't contacted in 30 days, and outputs a list of those names with their last contact date" produces working code. Teaches the technical brief structure: what it is, what it should do, what inputs it receives, what outputs it produces, and what constraints apply (language, file format, platform). Exercise (Tier 2): Provide a vague technical request. The learner rewrites it as a complete technical brief using the structure, then compares the AI output from both versions.

**Guide 2 — How to Understand and Fix Code Using AI**

The most common barrier after getting code from AI: it does not work, and the learner does not know why. Teaches the learner to paste error messages into AI with context and ask for a specific explanation, not just a fix. The difference matters: a fix they copy-paste teaches nothing and will fail again; an explanation they understand teaches them to avoid the same error. Also teaches the review prompt: "Read this code and explain what each section does in plain language." Exercise (Tier 2): Provide broken code with a specific error. The learner asks AI to explain the error (not fix it), then applies the explanation to write the fix themselves. They run the corrected code.

**Guide 3 — How to Build a Working Tool with AI**

End-to-end experience: from problem to working tool. The learner selects a real small problem in their own work (a spreadsheet calculation they do manually, a message they send repeatedly, a file they rename every week) and uses AI to build a tool that solves it. Covers: writing the initial brief, evaluating the first version, iterating on specific failures, and testing the final version against the original problem. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner builds a tool that solves a real problem they have. The tool must run and produce the correct output before the exercise is complete.

## Track Mission: Ship Something That Works

**Slug:** `ship-something-that-works`
**Deliverable:** A working tool — script, automation, web page, or application — that solves a real problem. Accompanied by a brief document: what the problem was, how the tool solves it, and what the learner would build next.
**Time:** 90 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` + `/earn/remote-jobs` — The ability to build working tools is one of the most in-demand and highest-paid skills in remote work. The Mission deliverable is a portfolio piece.
**Badge:** "Builder"

# Track 6: Automation

## Purpose

Automation is the discipline of making computers do repetitive work so humans do not have to.

Every Filipino professional who manages their own workload — freelancers, content creators, small business owners, virtual assistants — has tasks they do the same way every day, every week, every month.

This Track teaches the learner to identify those tasks, design automations for them, and build them — with AI as the architect and no-code tools as the construction material.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Spends time on tasks they know are repetitive and wasteful. Has heard of automation but believes it requires technical skills they do not have.

**Leaves:** Has automated at least one real task in their work. Looks at every repetitive task as a potential automation candidate.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `find-what-to-automate` | How to Find What Is Worth Automating | Concept | 5 min | Critical Thinking |
| 2 | `design-an-automation-with-ai` | How to Design an Automation Before You Build It | Skill | 10 min | Hands-On |
| 3 | `build-your-first-automation` | How to Build Your First Automation with AI and No-Code | Applied | 15 min | Experimentation |
| 4 | `chain-automations-together` | How to Connect Automations Into a System | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |

**Total: 4 Guides · 45 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Find What Is Worth Automating**

Not every repetitive task is worth automating. The learner needs a filter. Teaches the three-question test: Does this task happen regularly (daily, weekly, monthly)? Does it always follow the same steps? Would completing it faster or without my involvement save me real time? If the answer to all three is yes, it is an automation candidate. Uses AI to help the learner audit their own work and surface the best candidates. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner lists every task they did last week and uses AI to evaluate each against the three-question test. They end with a ranked list of automation candidates.

**Guide 2 — How to Design an Automation Before You Build It**

Automation built without design breaks. Design built without mapping breaks. Teaches the trigger-action-output model: what starts the automation (trigger), what steps it follows (actions), and what it produces (output). Uses AI to help the learner map their chosen automation in plain language before touching any tool. The design document becomes the build brief. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to map their top automation candidate into a full trigger-action-output document. Then they use AI to identify where the automation could break and add error handling to the design.

**Guide 3 — How to Build Your First Automation with AI and No-Code**

Takes the design document from Guide 2 and builds the automation using a no-code tool (Zapier, Make, n8n, or similar — the Guide is tool-type-agnostic). AI assists in two ways: helping the learner understand the no-code tool's interface through plain-language explanation, and debugging connection errors between steps. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner builds and successfully runs their top automation candidate end-to-end. The automation runs at least once before the exercise is complete.

**Guide 4 — How to Connect Automations Into a System**

One automation saves minutes. A system of connected automations saves hours. Teaches the learner to connect two automations together — where the output of the first triggers the second — producing a multi-step system that handles more of a workflow without human intervention. Uses AI to help design the connection logic and anticipate failures at the handoff point between automations. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner adds a second automation that is triggered by the output of the one they built in Guide 3 — or designs the connection on paper if the build requires more time than the exercise allows.

## Track Mission: Automate a Week of Work

**Slug:** `automate-a-week-of-work`
**Deliverable:** Two working automations — one single-step and one two-step system — plus a time audit showing how much time the automations save per week.
**Time:** 90 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` + `/earn/remote-jobs` — Automation skills command premium rates. Virtual assistants who automate client workflows retain clients longer and earn more. This Mission is the first automation portfolio piece.
**Badge:** "Automator"

# Track 7: Design

## Purpose

Visual communication is non-negotiable for anyone who wants to build an online presence, serve clients, or market their own work.

But professional design has always required expensive tools, trained eyes, and years of practice.

AI changes that equation.

Not by replacing designers — but by giving non-designers the ability to produce professional-quality visuals when they direct AI clearly, evaluate outputs critically, and iterate toward a standard they can define.

This Track teaches exactly that.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Can not produce visual work at a professional standard. Relies on free templates that look like everyone else's templates.

**Leaves:** Produces custom visual assets using AI tools — logos, social graphics, presentation slides, branded content — that meet a professional standard and look like they were made for a specific person or brand.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `brief-ai-like-a-designer` | How to Write a Visual Brief That Gets Good Results | Skill | 10 min | Prompt Practice |
| 2 | `build-visual-consistency` | How to Create Visual Assets That Look Like They Belong Together | Applied | 15 min | Experimentation |
| 3 | `iterate-on-design-with-ai` | How to Improve AI Design Until It Meets Your Standard | Skill | 10 min | Revision |

**Total: 3 Guides · 35 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Write a Visual Brief That Gets Good Results**

AI image tools produce generic results from generic prompts. A specific visual brief — describing mood, style, color direction, subject, context, and format — produces something designed, not just generated. Teaches the seven elements of a visual brief and how to use reference language that AI understands: style adjectives, color descriptions, composition language, format specifications. The learner also learns to specify what to exclude — which is often more important than specifying what to include. Exercise (Tier 2): Provide a weak visual prompt. The learner rewrites it as a full seven-element brief and runs both in an AI image tool. Compare and write: what specifically changed, and was the brief version better?

**Guide 2 — How to Create Visual Assets That Look Like They Belong Together**

Professional visual work has consistency: the same color palette, the same typographic style, the same compositional approach. Without consistency, even well-made individual assets look unprofessional as a set. Teaches the learner to use AI to define a simple visual identity (three colors, two font directions, one style descriptor) and then use that identity as the constant in every subsequent visual brief — so all assets produced in a session feel like they belong together. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner defines their visual identity (for their freelance brand, content channel, or business) using AI and produces three consistent assets — a profile image or logo, a social media post graphic, and a presentation slide — all from the same visual identity brief.

**Guide 3 — How to Improve AI Design Until It Meets Your Standard**

Getting a visual output from AI is the easy part. Getting one that is actually good enough to use is the harder skill. Teaches the learner to evaluate AI visual output against three standards: professional quality (would this embarrass me if I sent it to a client?), on-brief (does it match what I asked for?), and fit for purpose (does it serve the specific use case?). Then teaches the iteration prompt — specific language that adjusts one visual variable at a time without losing what was working. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner runs an AI image prompt, evaluates the output against three standards, identifies one specific weakness, and writes the iteration prompt that addresses only that weakness. They run the iteration and compare.

## Track Mission: Build Your Visual Kit

**Slug:** `build-your-visual-kit`
**Deliverable:** A complete visual kit — five consistent assets for a real use case (a freelance brand, a content channel, or a business): profile graphic, header image, post template, presentation slide, and one additional asset of the learner's choice.
**Time:** 60 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/freelancing` + `/earn/content-creation` — Design is the fastest-growing freelance category and a mandatory skill for creators. This Mission is a portfolio piece.
**Badge:** "Visual Pro"

# Track 8: Video

## Purpose

Video is the dominant communication format on every platform where Filipino professionals build audiences and find work.

Most people who want to create video content never start because they believe they need a studio, expensive equipment, or an editing background.

This Track teaches the learner to use AI to script, produce, and optimize video content with the equipment they already have — starting from their first video and building toward a repeatable content system.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Wants to create video content but has not started, or has started inconsistently without a process. Finds scripting, production, or optimization painful.

**Leaves:** Has a complete video production workflow powered by AI — from scripted idea to published video — and has published at least one complete piece of video content.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `script-a-video-with-ai` | How to Script a Video That Holds Attention | Skill | 10 min | Prompt Practice |
| 2 | `produce-faster-with-ai` | How to Produce Video Faster Using AI | Skill | 10 min | Hands-On |
| 3 | `optimize-your-video-with-ai` | How to Optimize Your Video Before and After Publishing | Applied | 10 min | Experimentation |
| 4 | `repurpose-video-with-ai` | How to Turn One Video Into Five Pieces of Content | Applied | 10 min | Hands-On |

**Total: 4 Guides · 40 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Script a Video That Holds Attention**

Most video content loses 70% of viewers in the first 30 seconds. The problem is almost always the hook or the lack of a clear structure. Teaches the three-part video script structure: the hook (why should I watch this?), the body (what exactly am I getting?), and the close (what should I do next?). Uses AI to generate script options from a topic and audience brief — then teaches the learner to evaluate and select the hook that works, not the one AI generated first. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner writes a topic and audience brief for a video they want to create, uses AI to generate three alternative hooks, selects the strongest one, and writes the reason for their choice.

**Guide 2 — How to Produce Video Faster Using AI**

Production is where most creators lose momentum. Teaches AI use at each stage of the production process: B-roll descriptions the learner can shoot with a phone, automated captions, AI tools that enhance audio quality, and background replacement for creators who do not have a clean filming environment. Platform-neutral — teaches the workflow, not specific software. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner produces a one-minute test video using only their phone and at least two AI production tools (captions, background, audio enhancement). The exercise ends when the video is exported and watchable.

**Guide 3 — How to Optimize Your Video Before and After Publishing**

A video that nobody finds is a video that does not exist for most people. Teaches AI-assisted optimization at two points: before publishing (title, thumbnail brief, description, tags, keywords) and after publishing (analyzing performance data to understand why a video performed as it did and what to test next). Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to write three alternative titles for a video, generate a thumbnail brief for a designer or an AI image tool, and write a 150-word description optimized for their platform's search.

**Guide 4 — How to Turn One Video Into Five Pieces of Content**

A single 5–10 minute video contains enough material for a week of content across multiple platforms. Teaches the repurposing workflow: transcript to short-form clips (identifying the three best 30–60 second moments), transcript to written article, key points to social captions, audio to podcast episode, and visual moments to still images. AI handles the transformation; the learner handles the selection. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner takes a completed video (their own or a provided sample), uses AI to identify the three best short-form clip moments, write a social caption for each, and extract a 300-word article from the transcript.

## Track Mission: Publish Your First Video Series

**Slug:** `publish-your-first-video-series`
**Deliverable:** Three complete, published videos — each using the script structure from Guide 1 — plus the repurposed content from each (captions, clips, or article). Nine total content assets from three videos.
**Time:** 90 minutes (plus production time — spread across multiple sessions)
**Earn Connection:** `/earn/content-creation` — Three published videos with repurposed content is the minimum viable start for a monetizable content channel or a video production service portfolio.
**Badge:** "On Camera"

# Track 9: Business

## Purpose

Most Filipinos who want to build a business or professional practice know what they want to offer.

They do not know how to make it real.

A business idea without a validation test is a guess. A service without a pricing strategy is underpriced. A professional practice without a client management system is chaotic.

This Track teaches the learner to use AI to move from business idea to operating business — validating the idea, pricing the service, writing the documents, and building the plan — in a sequence that mirrors how actual businesses are launched.

## Transformation

**Enters:** Has a business idea or professional ambition but does not know how to make it concrete, defensible, and operational.

**Leaves:** Has validated a business idea against real market evidence, set defensible prices, produced the professional documents their business needs, and has a 90-day plan they believe is executable.

## Guides

| # | Slug | Title | Type | Time | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `validate-a-business-idea` | How to Test a Business Idea Before You Invest in It | Skill | 10 min | Experimentation |
| 2 | `price-your-services-with-ai` | How to Set a Price You Can Defend | Skill | 10 min | Critical Thinking |
| 3 | `write-business-documents-with-ai` | How to Write the Documents Your Business Needs | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |
| 4 | `plan-your-first-90-days` | How to Plan Your First 90 Days with AI | Applied | 15 min | Hands-On |

**Total: 4 Guides · 50 minutes**

### Guide Designs

**Guide 1 — How to Test a Business Idea Before You Invest in It**

A business idea that nobody will pay for is not a business. It is an expensive hobby. Teaches the learner to use AI to stress-test their idea against four questions: who specifically would pay for this, what is the next-best alternative they currently use, why would they switch, and how much would they realistically pay? AI generates answers to each question from a market perspective — then the learner assesses whether those answers apply to real people they could talk to. The test ends with a confidence rating: proceed, pivot, or stop. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to stress-test their own business idea against the four questions and writes a one-paragraph assessment: is this idea ready to develop, or does something need to change first?

**Guide 2 — How to Set a Price You Can Defend**

Underpricing is the most common mistake Filipino freelancers and entrepreneurs make. It is also one of the most expensive — not just financially, but in terms of the quality of clients and work it attracts. Teaches the learner to use AI to research: what others charge for comparable services or products, what the market expects to pay at different quality levels, and how to communicate the value that justifies the price they want to charge. The learner leaves with a pricing range and a one-sentence value statement that explains why their price is what it is. Exercise (Tier 2): The learner uses AI to research three comparable services or products, identify the pricing range, position their offering within it, and write the value statement.

**Guide 3 — How to Write the Documents Your Business Needs**

Every business eventually needs the same set of documents: a service agreement or contract, a proposal template, an invoice, an FAQ for prospective clients, and a professional bio. Teaches the learner to use AI to produce all five — with their specific service, rate, and terms as the inputs. Teaches what each document must contain, what common mistakes to avoid, and what language to add or remove for a Filipino freelancing context. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner produces all five documents using AI — with their own real information as the input — and reviews them for completeness against a provided checklist.

**Guide 4 — How to Plan Your First 90 Days with AI**

A business without a 90-day plan is a business that reacts. A business with one is a business that executes. Teaches the 90-day planning framework: the first 30 days are about setting up (systems, accounts, documents, presence), the second 30 days are about finding (first clients, first audience, first revenue), and the third 30 days are about delivering (executing the work, collecting feedback, and deciding what to scale). AI generates specific weekly actions for each phase from the learner's own situation. The learner pressure-tests the plan and rewrites the final version by hand. Exercise (Tier 3): The learner runs the 90-day planning workflow from AI Workflows Pillar (if completed) or uses the structure provided. The final plan is written by the learner, not pasted from AI.

**Time:** 120 minutes
**Earn Connection:** `/earn` (general) — The starter kit is the foundation of any Earn path that involves operating as an independent professional.
**Badge:** "In Business"

# Overlap Review

## Resolved: Domain skill instruction vs. workflow instruction

**Risk:** Every domain Guide could become a workflow Guide — re-teaching what Pillar 4 already covered.

**Resolution:** Domain Guides teach judgment within the domain — what makes good SEO writing, what a winning proposal must contain, what design consistency means. Pillar 4 teaches the workflow mechanics — phases, handoffs, evaluation. These are different layers of the same activity. If a Guide sentence begins "in step two of this workflow..." it has drifted into Pillar 4. If it begins "a winning proposal must include..." it belongs here.

## Resolved: Writing Track vs. Content Workflow (Pillar 4)

**Risk:** The Writing Track overlaps with the Writing Workflow taught in AI Workflows Guide 6.

**Resolution:** AI Workflows Guide 6 taught the four-step writing workflow as a generic process — applicable to any writing task. The Writing Track in this Pillar teaches specific writing formats with their own structural rules: what a professional email must contain, what a proposal structure looks like, what makes a voice recognizable. The domain specificity is what separates them. Guide writers for the Writing Track must teach what makes each format work — not how to run the four-step workflow. That workflow is assumed and linked.

## Resolved: Marketing Track vs. AI Workflows Planning Workflow

**Risk:** The Marketing Track Guide 3 (content plan) overlaps with the Planning Workflow in AI Workflows Guide 8.

**Resolution:** AI Workflows Guide 8 taught how to turn a goal into a plan using the planning workflow mechanics. Marketing Track Guide 3 teaches marketing-specific content strategy: audience journey stages, content types for each stage, and how to build a plan that serves a marketing goal — not just any goal. The domain knowledge about audience journeys and content types belongs exclusively to the Marketing Track.

## Resolved: Programming Track vs. Automation Track

**Risk:** Programming and Automation overlap — both involve building things with code or code-adjacent tools.

**Resolution:** Programming teaches the learner to produce code — scripts, functions, applications — using AI as the coding partner. The learner's skill is directing AI to write code correctly and debugging it when it fails. Automation teaches the learner to design and connect no-code automation systems — where the tools are visual platforms, not code editors. The learner's skill is identifying what to automate, designing the trigger-action-output logic, and connecting tools without writing code. A learner might complete both Tracks — the Programming Track makes Automation Track work more powerful, and vice versa — but the skills are distinct.

## Resolved: Design Track vs. Content Workflow (Pillar 4)

**Risk:** The Design Track (producing visual assets) could overlap with the Content Workflow in AI Workflows Guide 7, which includes a visual brief step.

**Resolution:** AI Workflows Guide 7 taught the content creation workflow — where visual brief writing appears briefly as one step in a larger process. The Design Track teaches visual brief writing as its primary skill (Guide 1), visual consistency as a professional standard (Guide 2), and design iteration as a craft (Guide 3). The depth and domain specificity of the Design Track far exceed the brief mention in Pillar 4. There is no meaningful overlap.

## Resolved: Business Track vs. Planning Workflow (Pillar 4)

**Risk:** Business Track Guide 4 (90-day plan) directly uses the planning workflow from AI Workflows Guide 8.

**Resolution:** The connection is explicit and intentional — Business Track Guide 4 references AI Workflows Guide 8 as the planning workflow to use. What the Business Track adds is the business-specific framework: the three 30-day phases (set up, find, deliver) and the criteria for each phase. These are domain decisions, not workflow mechanics. The workflow is the vehicle. The business framework is the destination.

# Notes for Guide Writers

1. AI-first means the domain skill and the AI use develop simultaneously. There is no "first learn the domain, then add AI" phase in any Guide in this Pillar. If a Guide begins with domain theory before reaching AI, rewrite it. The AI enters at the first sentence of the exercise.

2. Every Track Mission must produce something the learner can use or show immediately. The Website Creation Mission produces a live website. The Writing Mission produces documents ready to send. The Video Mission produces published content. The Business Mission produces operating documents. If the Mission deliverable is "a draft" or "a document," revise the Mission until the deliverable is real.

3. The learner's own situation is the non-negotiable input to every Tier 3 exercise. A learner doing the Programming Mission builds a tool for their own real problem. A learner doing the Business Mission builds a starter kit for their actual business idea. Generic scenarios are acceptable only in Tier 1 and Tier 2 exercises — never at Tier 3.

4. Tracks must not assume prior domain knowledge but may assume AI collaboration skills. A learner in the Design Track who does not know the principles of color theory can still complete the Track — because AI handles the color theory. A learner in the Writing Track who is not a confident writer can still complete the Track — because the workflow scaffolds the writing. Every Guide should be accessible to someone for whom the domain is new.

5. The SEO Track, Marketing Track, and Content Workflow from Pillar 4 are the most cross-referenced pieces of curriculum on the platform. Guide writers for these Tracks must read the Pillar 4 workflow documents carefully and ensure their domain-specific content does not duplicate or contradict the workflow mechanics taught there.

6. Track Missions for the Video Track require the learner to publish. "Published" means live on a real platform — YouTube, TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook — not saved in a folder. The act of publishing is part of the learning. If a learner is not ready to publish publicly, they may post as unlisted or draft — but they must post. A video that was never posted is not a Mission completion.

7. The Business Track is the most sensitive Track to write well. Filipino learners entering this Track may be attempting to build their first source of income from an idea they are emotionally invested in. Guides must be honest about validation — if the idea does not pass the stress-test in Guide 1, the Guide must help the learner understand that clearly, without discouraging them from refining the idea. The goal is a business that works, not a business that feels good to plan.
